## About this video

Buying a first home is an exciting milestone, but the process can feel overwhelming. In this explainer, Kirby Chan, Broker, of Kirby Chan & Co. Real Estate Team walks through what first-time buyers need to know in Richmond Hill, Markham and Toronto.

He also explains how mentorship, clear guidance and a trusted network of lenders and inspectors can make the process simpler.

## What a first purchase costs in Toronto

The minimum down payment depends on the price, not on who is buying: 5% of the first $500,000, 10% of the part above $500,000 and 20% of the whole price from $1.5 million. On the City of Toronto median of $770,000 in August 2026 (TRREB), that is $52,000.

Since December 15, 2024, insured mortgages have been available on homes up to $1.5 million and first-time buyers with less than 20% down can take a 30-year amortization. A longer amortization lowers the monthly payment and raises the interest paid overall.

## Two land transfer taxes and two rebates

Buying in the City of Toronto means paying Ontario land transfer tax and the City's municipal land transfer tax. On $770,000 that is $11,875 each or $23,750 together. An eligible first-time buyer can recover up to $4,000 from Ontario and up to $4,475 from the City, which brings that purchase to $15,275.

Savings can come from a First Home Savings Account, which holds up to $40,000 over your lifetime and the RRSP Home Buyers' Plan, which allows up to $60,000 to be withdrawn and repaid over 15 years. Our [first-time home buyer guide for Toronto](/first-time-home-buyers-toronto/) sets the rules side by side and the post on the [FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan](/blog/fhsa-and-home-buyers-plan-toronto/) explains the savings tools.
