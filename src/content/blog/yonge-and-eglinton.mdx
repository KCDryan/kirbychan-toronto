## The short answer

Yonge and Eglinton is the Midtown Toronto area around Eglinton station, where Line 1 Yonge University meets Line 5 Eglinton. The towers at the corner and the house streets a few blocks away are governed by one City document, the Yonge-Eglinton Secondary Plan, which decides where tall buildings can go and where they cannot.

For a buyer, that plan explains what is likely to change around a condo or a house and what new condo buildings are asked to include. This post walks through it in plain terms, with what the TTC and the City publish about the area today.

Prices by property type, the 2021 Census housing stock and the parks are in our [guide to living in Yonge and Eglinton](/yonge-eglinton-toronto/). Current listings are on our [Yonge and Eglinton homes for sale](/homes-for-sale/yonge-eglinton/) page.

## Where Yonge and Eglinton begins and ends

The City's planning area is larger than the intersection. City Planning describes the Secondary Plan area as about six square kilometres around Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue, generally bounded by:

- Briar Hill Avenue and Blythwood Road to the north
- Mount Pleasant Cemetery to the south
- Bayview Avenue to the east
- Chaplin Crescent and Latimer Avenue to the west

Inside it is the Yonge-Eglinton Centre, which the City calls one of four Centres in its Official Plan. It is also an Urban Growth Centre in the Province's Growth Plan for the Greater Golden Horseshoe. The City's Midtown Zoning Review page counts over 72,000 residents in the 2021 Census and 33,000 jobs across the plan area.

That boundary takes in more than one of our neighbourhood guides. The streets between Mount Pleasant Road and Bayview Avenue are covered in our [Mount Pleasant East guide](/mount-pleasant-east-toronto/) and the house streets to the north in our [Lawrence Park guide](/lawrence-park-toronto/). ## The Yonge-Eglinton Secondary Plan in plain terms

City Council adopted the Yonge-Eglinton Secondary Plan as Official Plan Amendment 405 in July 2018. The Province approved it, with modifications, in June 2019. The City describes it as a 25-year vision with a policy framework to guide intensification and priority infrastructure improvements.

The Official Plan text that came with it says the Centre will be marked by tall buildings and an intense concentration of office, retail, institutional and residential uses at the Yonge-Eglinton intersection and that the scale and intensity of development will transition down in all directions. It names Mount Pleasant station as a secondary focal point in the east end.

### The five groups of Character Areas

The plan sorts its growth areas into five groups, each split into smaller Character Areas with their own direction:

| Group | What the plan describes |
| --- | --- |
| Midtown Cores | Mixed-use areas centred on the transit stations: the Yonge-Eglinton Crossroads, Davisville Station, Mount Pleasant Station and Bayview Focus |
| Midtown Villages | Historic main streets with low and mid-rise buildings and shops at street level, such as Eglinton Way and Mount Pleasant South |
| Midtown Apartment Neighbourhoods | Apartment towers, walk-ups and townhouses in landscaped settings, such as Soudan and Davisville |
| Midtown Apartment High Streets | Residential stretches of major streets between the Villages and Cores, such as Eglinton Park and Mount Pleasant Gateway |
| Midtown Special Places | Office, institutional and civic destinations, such as Montgomery Square and Merton Street |

Building heights peak at the Crossroads. Elsewhere the plan asks for less: tall buildings of a modest height alongside mid-rise buildings around Mount Pleasant station and generally low-rise walk-up apartment buildings to fill gaps in the Eglinton Park stretch of Eglinton Avenue West.

The Character Areas apply to the plan's growth areas and Map 21-2 shows where each one is. A house on a quiet side street can sit a block from a Core or a Village, so check that map for the exact address.

## What the plan asks of new condo buildings

The housing section is the part a condo buyer is most likely to notice. To support housing suitable for families, the plan as approved in 2019 says a development with more than 80 new residential units will include:

1. at least 15 per cent of units as two-bedroom units of 87 square metres or more
2. at least 10 per cent as three-bedroom units of 100 square metres or more
3. a further 15 per cent as two or three-bedroom units or units that can be converted to them

The City may reduce these minimums for social or publicly funded housing and for specialized housing, such as residences run by a post-secondary or health care institution. Plans can be amended after approval, so ask City Planning or your lawyer to confirm the current wording for a building that interests you.

For an existing condo, the rule tells you nothing about a specific unit. Measure that unit the same way you would anywhere else. Before you firm up, read the [condo status certificate](/blog/condo-status-certificate-toronto/) with your lawyer. Buyers looking at a building that is not yet finished can compare the protections in our [new construction guide](/new-construction-toronto/).

## Midtown zoning: what is in force

A Secondary Plan sets the direction. Zoning by-laws set the detailed rules on use, height, density, building spacing, parking and loading. The City's Midtown Zoning Review has brought in new zoning in stages:

| When | What City Council or the Tribunal did |
| --- | --- |
| June 2022 | Council adopted zoning for commercial residential areas and revised zoning for five Villages Character Areas |
| May 2023 | Council adopted rezoning for selected low-rise areas |
| February 7, 2024 | Council adopted Zoning By-law 63-2024 for lands designated Midtown Apartment Neighbourhoods |
| August 2024 | An Ontario Land Tribunal decision brought By-law 595-2022 into force, except for certain properties still under appeal |

The City notes that the zoning for the Midtown Villages is in force with limited site-specific exceptions still under appeal. If a property's zoning matters to your plans, have it confirmed for that address rather than relying on a summary.

## Parks, streets and infrastructure the City has planned

The plan also commits the area to a set of public realm projects. The Midtown Parks and Public Realm Plan of 2018 identified eleven Public Realm Moves and the Secondary Plan lists them:

- the Eglinton Green Line
- Yonge Street Squares and the Yonge Street Squares Extension
- the Park Street Loop and the Midtown Greenways
- Redpath Revisited and the Davisville Community Street
- the Mount Pleasant Road Arboretum and the Merton Street Promenade
- the Balliol Green Street and the Pailton Crescent Connector

The Eglinton Green Line runs along Eglinton Avenue from Eglinton Park to Mount Pleasant Road. The plan asks buildings on the north side of Eglinton Avenue between Yonge Street and Mount Pleasant Road to be set back at least 12 metres from the property line to make room for landscaped open space. City Council adopted the Green Line landscape and public realm standards in July 2021.

Behind the streetscape, City Council adopted the Midtown Infrastructure Implementation Strategy in June 2022. The City says its sewer work focuses on combined and storm sewers in parts of Midtown to address existing basement flooding risk. If you are buying a house, ask the seller about any water in the basement and ask your home inspector and insurer what they look for.

## Transit at Eglinton station

Eglinton station at 2190 Yonge Street serves Line 1 and Line 5. The TTC lists it as accessible, with 8 elevators. The Line 5 platform has elevator access directly to the Line 1 platform level. The station has no parking.

The TTC lists these bus routes at the station: 13 Avenue Rd, 34 Eglinton, 61 Avenue Rd North, 74 Mount Pleasant, 97 Yonge, 103 Mount Pleasant North, 320 Yonge, 334 Eglinton and 354 Lawrence East.

Line 5 Eglinton opened on Sunday, February 8, 2026. The TTC's opening timetables list 25 stations from Mount Dennis to Kennedy and the TTC said eastbound trains would depart about every 7 to 8 minutes through the day. Our post on the [Toronto subway commute by neighbourhood](/blog/toronto-subway-commute-by-neighbourhood/) counts the stops to Union from Eglinton station and the other stations in our guides.

For everyday errands, the Northern District branch of the Toronto Public Library is at 40 Orchard View Boulevard. TPL says it was its first district branch and lists a youth hub, a computer learning centre and a local history collection.

## Heritage districts in Midtown

The City's list of heritage conservation districts names none around the intersection itself. The Historic Yonge Street district covers Yonge Street between College Street and Bloor Street, well to the south. The Blythwood Road district, designated in 2005, is on the list and our Mount Pleasant East guide places it in the northern part of that neighbourhood. Individual properties can be designated outside any district, so check the exact address with the City before you plan exterior work.

## What to check before you buy at Yonge and Eglinton

1. Find the address on the Secondary Plan's Map 21-2 and note which Character Area it is in, if any.
2. Search the address and its neighbours in the City's [Application Information Centre](https://www.toronto.ca/city-government/planning-development/application-information-centre/) for active planning applications, Committee of Adjustment matters and Toronto Local Appeal Body appeals.
3. For a condo, ask whether the view or the light depends on a neighbouring site that could be redeveloped.
4. For a newer building, ask the seller or the property manager what size of units the building was approved with.
5. For a house, ask about past basement water and have the zoning confirmed if you plan to extend or rebuild. Our post on [Willowdale real estate](/blog/willowdale-real-estate/) walks through the City's rebuild steps.
6. Confirm school boundaries for the exact address with the TDSB or TCDSB.
