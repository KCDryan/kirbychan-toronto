## The short answer

Riverdale Toronto real estate and The Beaches both draw families looking for a larger house in the east end. Both are house neighbourhoods, both have older streets and both have a heritage conservation district. The clearest difference is how you get around.

Riverdale sits on Line 2 Bloor-Danforth, with Broadview, Chester, Pape and Donlands stations along its north edge. The Beaches has no subway station. Its residents ride the Queen and Kingston Road streetcars or take a bus north to Line 2.

This post compares the two on the things that matter once you own the house: the daily trip, the heritage rules and the City's tree and ravine bylaws. For prices by property type, our full guides to [Riverdale](/riverdale-toronto/) and [The Beaches](/the-beaches-toronto/) carry the latest quarterly figures and the current listings are on our [Riverdale homes for sale](/homes-for-sale/riverdale/) and [The Beaches homes for sale](/homes-for-sale/the-beaches/) pages.

## Where each neighbourhood sits

Riverdale lies just east of the Don River. The City describes its heritage district as containing some of the earliest properties on the east side of the Don. Our Riverdale guide reports North Riverdale and South Riverdale separately, so check which one a listing falls in before you compare figures.

The Beaches runs along Lake Ontario further east. The Kingswood Road South district plan calls the Balmy Beach area a former seasonal resort community.

Both areas sit south of Line 2, which is why the subway question shapes so much of the comparison. Our [map of Toronto](/map-of-toronto/) shows the two side by side.

## Getting downtown: Line 2 stations or the Queen streetcar

### Riverdale

Four Line 2 stations serve Riverdale. Broadview station is at 760 Broadview Avenue and has its own platform for the 505 Dundas streetcar. Pape station is at 743 Pape Avenue and connects with the 72 Pape and 83 Jones buses. Both stations are listed by the TTC as accessible, with elevators to each platform.

Counted on the TTC map, Broadview is 3 stops west of Bloor-Yonge. Change to Line 1 there and ride 6 stops south to Union. The 501 Queen, 504 King, 505 Dundas and 506 Carlton streetcar routes also cross the area.

### The Beaches

The 501 Queen and 503 Kingston Rd streetcars share Queen Street East, with the 503 turning up Kingston Road toward Bingham Loop. For the subway, the 92 Woodbine South bus connects with Woodbine station at 991 Woodbine Avenue and the 64 Main bus with Main Street station at 315 Main Street. Main Street station also connects to Danforth GO station at 213 Main Street, on the Lakeshore East and Stouffville lines.

For a household with two commuters, that bus leg is the real difference. A Riverdale house a short walk from Line 2 removes it. In The Beaches the walk to a streetcar stop on Queen Street East or Kingston Road decides the routine. ## Heritage conservation districts in each area

A heritage conservation district is an area the City protects under Part V of the Ontario Heritage Act. The City says new construction, exterior alterations and additions to properties inside a district need a heritage permit. Work that is not visible from the street, interior work and routine maintenance do not. Each district has its own plan with its own rules.

| | Riverdale | The Beaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| District | Riverdale | Kingswood Road South |
| Year designated | 2008 | 2010 |
| What the City says | Some of the earliest properties on the east side of the Don River | Part of the Balmy Beach neighbourhood, a former seasonal resort community |
| Extent | Set out in the district plan | Kingswood Road between Bracken Avenue and Queen Street East, addresses 4 to 175 |

For a move up buyer this matters most when the plan is to add space. An addition or an exterior change to a house inside a district can need a heritage permit as well as a building permit and the district plan says which. Read it before you firm up an offer and allow time for the review.

Outside the districts, the usual zoning and building permit rules apply. Neither district covers the whole neighbourhood, so the exact address decides.

## Trees, ravines and the land around the house

Larger lots in older neighbourhoods often come with mature trees and that brings City rules into any renovation plan. These apply across Toronto, so they are not a reason to choose one area over the other. They are a reason to look closely at a particular lot in either.

The City requires a permit to injure or remove a private tree with a diameter of 30 cm or more. Diameter is worked out from the circumference measured 1.4 metres above the ground. Street trees owned by the City are protected at any size and some sit in front yards next to the sidewalk, so check a survey or call 311 if you are unsure who owns a tree.

Ravines and natural features carry stricter rules. Inside a protected area you cannot harm or remove a tree of any size, change the topography, place fill or build structures or retaining walls without a permit. The City's Ravine and Natural Feature Protection By-law Map shows whether an address is affected, so check it for any house that backs onto parkland or a slope.

## Everyday life: libraries and amenities

Each neighbourhood has its own Toronto Public Library branch. The Riverdale branch is at 370 Broadview Avenue and the Beaches branch is at 2161 Queen Street East.

Our neighbourhood guides list the parks, recreation centres and other branches in each area. If a particular school matters to you, confirm the boundary for the exact address with the TDSB or TCDSB, because boundaries are set by each board.

## What to check before you move up

1. Time the trip from the exact address at the hour you travel, by subway in Riverdale or by streetcar and bus in The Beaches.
2. Check whether the house is inside the Riverdale or Kingswood Road South heritage district.
3. Look up the address on the City's ravine and natural feature map.
4. Note any tree of 30 cm or more near where you would build.
5. Confirm school boundaries with the TDSB or TCDSB for that address.
