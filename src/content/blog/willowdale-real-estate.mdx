## The short answer

Willowdale real estate is two markets on either side of Yonge Street: condo towers close to the subway and detached houses on the side streets further out. For a family moving up, a detached lot can be bought for what can be built on it as much as for the house standing there today.

Before you firm up an offer on a house you plan to replace or add to, three City steps decide what you can do and when. You confirm which zoning by-law applies to the lot. You apply to the Committee of Adjustment if the design breaks one of its rules. Then you get a building permit and, for a rebuild, a demolition permit with conditions attached.

This post walks through each step with the City's 2026 fees. For prices by property type, the housing stock and parks, see our full [guide to Willowdale](/willowdale-toronto/). Current listings are on our [Willowdale homes for sale](/homes-for-sale/willowdale/) page.

## Willowdale real estate: where the detached houses are

TRREB reports the area as two communities, Willowdale East and Willowdale West. Our guide carries the latest quarterly figures for each and they differ enough that a listing should only be compared with its own community.

The detached streets sit back from the Yonge Street corridor and its three Line 1 stations: Finch, North York Centre and Sheppard-Yonge. Sheppard-Yonge station at 20 Sheppard Avenue West is the interchange with Line 4 Sheppard and the TTC lists it as accessible, with bus routes including the 84 Sheppard West, 97 Yonge and 98 Willowdale-Senlac. ## Check the zoning before you make an offer

Zoning By-law 569-2013 is the City's single set of rules on how land may be used and how buildings are designed and placed. City Council first adopted it on May 9, 2013. It does not cover every lot. The City says some properties are still subject to the zoning by-laws of the municipalities that existed before amalgamation.

Willowdale is a place where that matters. It was part of the former City of North York and a City decision report dated January 31, 2025 for a site in Ward 18 Willowdale found the land still zoned under the former City of North York Zoning By-law No. 7625. That site was commercial, but the lesson applies to houses too: check which by-law governs the exact address.

To check a lot:

1. Search the address on the City's interactive zoning map.
2. If the property is shaded grey, it is subject to a former municipal zoning by-law, not 569-2013.
3. If it is covered by 569-2013, read the zone's permitted uses and standards and any site-specific exception in Chapter 900.

The City notes that its online map and text are for convenience and that the certified documents held by the City Clerk govern for legal purposes.

### The Zoning Applicable Law Certificate

Once you have drawings, the City will review them for zoning and other applicable law before you apply for a building permit. The Zoning Applicable Law Certificate includes up to three reviews. From January 1, 2026 the fee for a new house of up to two units or an addition is $644.38. It is not refundable, but it is deducted from the building permit fees if you file the declaration form with the permit application.

## When the plan needs a minor variance

If the design does not meet the zoning, the usual route is the Committee of Adjustment, an independent tribunal created by City Council. The City gives small changes to building setback or parking requirements as examples of minor variances. To approve one, the Committee must be satisfied of four things:

- the general intent and purpose of the Official Plan is maintained
- the general intent and purpose of the Zoning By-law is maintained
- the proposal is appropriate for the development of the land or building
- the variance is minor

### Minor variance fees in 2026

| Application | City fee from January 1, 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Additions and alterations to an existing dwelling with three units or less | $2,228.98 |
| Residential dwelling with three units or less | $5,011.08 |
| All other residential, commercial, industrial or institutional uses | $6,485.59 |
| Variance applied for after the work is done | Double the regular fee |

Applications go by email as PDFs to bldapplications@toronto.ca with the property address in the subject line. From October 1, 2026 the application is split into two forms, Form A and Form B. Both are required and the City stopped accepting the old form after October 31, 2026. There is no refund once the Notice of Hearing has been mailed.

### The North York hearing

Ward 18 Willowdale is on the list for the Committee's North York panel. Hearings are held at the North York Civic Centre, 5100 Yonge Street, in a hybrid format, so applicants and neighbours can attend in person or on Webex. The City's schedule lists November 26 and December 10 as the remaining 2026 dates.

For a minor variance, the City mails notice to owners within 60 metres of the property at least 10 days before the hearing and a notice sign must be posted on the property for 10 days. Most appeals go to the Toronto Local Appeal Body. If nobody appeals, the decision becomes final and binding.

### Dividing a lot

Splitting one lot into two needs a consent, which the same Committee decides. Notice for a consent is 14 days, both by mail to owners within 60 metres and by a sign on the property.

## Building and demolition permits for a new house

A building permit is required to construct or demolish a building, to build an addition and for any material alteration. For a rebuild, the City's guide says to apply for the demolition permit after you have submitted the permit for the new house, using the same channel (online or email). The demolition application needs:

- a survey or a grading plan showing the existing house, the property lines and the lot area
- an Infill Public Notice Declaration form
- an Owner's Acknowledgement of Demolition Control Conditions
- a Tree Declaration form where private or City trees could be damaged

Trees often decide where a new house can sit on a lot. Our post on [Riverdale Toronto real estate](/blog/riverdale-toronto-real-estate/) explains the City's permit for private trees of 30 cm or more and the ravine rules, which apply across the city.

### The two year rule

The whole City of Toronto is a demolition control area under Chapter 363 of the Municipal Code. For a residential property with fewer than six dwelling units outside the former City of Toronto, where a building permit for the new building has been issued, the demolition permit is issued on two conditions:

1. The new building must be substantially complete within two years of the day demolition of the existing house starts.
2. If it is not, the City Clerk can add $20,000 for each dwelling unit to the tax roll, collected like property taxes and held as a lien on the land until paid.

Inside the former City of Toronto the same conditions apply, but the applicant must also post a notice for at least 14 days before the City considers the application. Confirm with Toronto Building which rule applies to your address. If the existing house contains rental units, the Chief Building Official also checks whether the City's rental demolition and conversion rules in Chapter 667 apply.

## Adding on instead of rebuilding

An addition avoids demolition control but still needs a building permit. The City lists second or third storey additions, attached garages, sunrooms and porches, decks more than 60 centimetres above the ground and adding or removing walls among the work that needs one. Even where no permit is needed, the City says the work must still comply with the zoning by-law.

If the addition needs a variance, the lower $2,228.98 fee applies. To add a garden suite, a laneway suite or extra units instead, our [High Park Toronto real estate](/blog/high-park-toronto-real-estate/) post covers the City's rules on multiplexes and suites, which apply in Willowdale too.

## What to check before you buy in Willowdale

1. Look up the lot on the City's interactive zoning map and note whether it is shaded grey.
2. Ask a designer whether your plan meets the zoning or will need a variance and budget the fee.
3. Walk the lot for large trees near where you would build.
4. Ask whether the house has rental units, which can bring in Chapter 667.
5. Build the two year completion rule into your financing and your move.
6. Confirm school boundaries for the exact address with the TDSB or TCDSB.
