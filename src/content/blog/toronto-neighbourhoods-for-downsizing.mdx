## The short answer

The Toronto neighbourhoods for downsizing that offer the most choice are the ones with the most apartment stock. From April to June 2026, TRREB recorded the largest numbers of condo apartment sales among our twelve guides in the downtown waterfront, Willowdale East, Mount Pleasant West (part of Yonge and Eglinton), Islington-City Centre West, Bayview Village and the Annex. Every one of them has a subway or Line 5 Eglinton station.

The areas known for houses, such as Leaside and Lawrence Park, had very few condo sales in the same quarter. Riverdale and the Beaches offer a different kind of smaller home, with more semi-detached sales than condo apartment sales.

This post sits under our [Toronto downsizing guide](/downsizing-toronto/), which covers the whole move. Here we look at one question only: where smaller homes actually trade and what they cost, with the transit nearby.

## Where condo apartments concentrate

The biggest condo apartment markets in our twelve guides are along the subway lines. Waterfront Communities C1 alone had 396 condo apartment sales in the quarter at a median of about $607,000, with Union station inside the area.

Willowdale East had 153 condo apartment sales at about $570,000, along Line 1 between Sheppard-Yonge and Finch. Mount Pleasant West, the condo side of Yonge and Eglinton, had 114 at about $603,000, close to Eglinton station where Line 1 meets Line 5.

Islington-City Centre West had 109 condo apartment sales at about $518,000, the lowest median in the table. TRREB's community includes the Etobicoke City Centre condo area near Islington and Kipling stations on Line 2. Bayview Village had 83 at about $535,000 along Line 4 Sheppard. Don Mills had 50 at about $638,000 near its three Line 5 stations.

The Annex is the exception on price. Its 82 condo apartment sales had a median of about $858,000, well above the other large condo markets here, in an area served by both Line 1 and Line 2.

## Where condo townhouses and semi-detached houses sell

Condo townhouses are scarce in all twelve areas. Willowdale East had the most with 22 sales at about $1,025,000, then Bayview Village with 15 at about $775,000 and South Riverdale with 11 at about $705,000. Most other areas had three or fewer.

Semi-detached houses are the main smaller-house option in the east end and the older core. South Riverdale had 40 semi-detached sales at about $1,464,000, North Riverdale 26 at about $1,780,000 and the Beaches 23 at about $1,350,000. The Annex had 27 at about $1,725,000.

A semi-detached house still has stairs and a yard to look after, so it suits a different goal from a condo apartment. Our comparison of [bungalows, condos and townhouses for downsizing](/blog/bungalow-condo-or-townhouse-downsizing-toronto/) goes through what each home type involves day to day.

## Staying in a house neighbourhood: Leaside and Lawrence Park

Owners selling a detached house in Leaside or Lawrence Park often want to stay close by. The figures show why that is hard. Leaside had 11 condo apartment sales in the quarter and no condo townhouse sales. Lawrence Park North had 2 condo apartment sales and Lawrence Park South had 9, at a median of about $1,100,000.

Semi-detached houses give a little more choice: 10 sales in Leaside at about $1,455,000 and 10 in Lawrence Park North at about $1,700,000.

Transit is the bridge. Leaside station is 2 stops from Eglinton station on Line 5. Don Mills and the Yonge and Eglinton condo areas sit on the same line. Lawrence station is on Line 1, with Eglinton station at the south edge of Lawrence Park. Widening a search along those lines adds far more units than staying within one area. We compare the two in more detail in [Leaside vs Lawrence Park](/blog/leaside-vs-lawrence-park/).

## Reading these figures with care

- One quarter is a small sample. The Beaches' condo median rests on 7 sales and Yonge-Eglinton's semi-detached median on 3.
- A median describes the middle sale, not the unit you would buy. Size, floor, age of building and condo fees vary a great deal.
- TRREB's community boundaries and the City's Census boundaries differ, which is why some rows above name more than one area.
- Census figures are from 2021. Buildings finished since then are not in them.
