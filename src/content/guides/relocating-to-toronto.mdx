## Who this guide is for

Many people who move to Toronto rent for years. This guide is for households who expect to buy, on arrival or after a year here. The first section applies to everyone.

## Two deadlines in the first weeks

**Health coverage.** Ontario states that there is no longer a waiting period for OHIP and that eligible applicants have immediate coverage. You do not wait out 153 days before applying. You apply in person at a ServiceOntario centre with a completed registration form and three separate original documents: one for citizenship or OHIP-eligible immigration status, one for Ontario residency and one for identity.

Eligibility has continuing conditions. You need to be physically in Ontario for 153 days in any 12 month period and for at least 153 of your first 183 days here. Ontario must also be your primary residence. You also need one additional status, such as citizenship, permanent residence or full-time work in Ontario on a valid work permit for at least six months.

**Driving.** A valid licence from another province, state or country can be used for 60 days after you move. After that you need an Ontario licence.

Licences from Canadian provinces, U.S. states and a listed set of countries can be exchanged. From July 1, 2026, drivers from other countries can get credit for up to one year of experience with their original licence and an official authentication letter from the issuing government in English or French. Arrange that letter before you leave.

## Renting first or buying on arrival

For households that do not know Toronto, we usually suggest renting for a year before buying. The reasons are specific to this city.

Toronto covers a large area and its neighbourhoods do not blend into one another. A street of detached houses and a block of condo towers can sit a short walk apart. Daily travel depends heavily on which subway or light rail line is nearby. That only becomes clear once you have lived the commute.

Renting has costs: a second move and whatever prices do while you wait. If you arrive with a fixed workplace, a clear budget and a firm idea of the housing you need, buying on arrival is a reasonable decision.

## Getting around on the TTC

Where you live relative to rapid transit shapes your week more than almost anything else. The network, as the TTC names its lines:

- **Line 1 Yonge University** runs north and south through the centre and serves Union Station.
- **Line 2 Bloor Danforth** crosses the city east to west.
- **Line 4 Sheppard** runs east from Sheppard-Yonge along Sheppard Avenue.
- **Line 5 Eglinton** began service on February 8, 2026. It is 19 kilometres of light rail along Eglinton Avenue from Mount Dennis to Kennedy, with an interchange at Eglinton Station on Line 1.
- **Line 6 Finch West** opened on December 7, 2025, running between Humber College and Finch West.

Line 5 changed the options for several established areas. Leaside now has Leaside and Laird stations on Eglinton Avenue East. Don Mills Road has Don Valley Station. Elsewhere, the Annex sits on both Line 1 and Line 2 at Spadina and St George, Willowdale is on Line 1 at North York Centre and Finch, Bayview Village is on Line 4 and the Beaches rely on the 501 Queen streetcar. Our post on [subway commutes by neighbourhood](/blog/toronto-subway-commute-by-neighbourhood/) and the page on [neighbourhoods for commuters](/best-toronto-neighbourhoods-for-commuters/) go further.

## The tax picture when you buy

**Two land transfer taxes.** Ontario charges land transfer tax across the province. The City of Toronto charges its own municipal land transfer tax on property in the city, on top of the provincial tax. On a $770,000 purchase each is $11,875, so the total is $23,750. Toronto's municipal tax does not apply to purchases in Markham, Mississauga or Vaughan. See [Toronto vs Markham](/toronto-vs-markham/), [Toronto vs Mississauga](/toronto-vs-mississauga/) and [Toronto vs Vaughan](/toronto-vs-vaughan/) if you are weighing a nearby city.

**First-time buyer rebates.** Ontario refunds up to $4,000 and Toronto rebates up to $4,475 for eligible first-time buyers. Both require citizenship or permanent residence, which can be gained up to 18 months after the transfer. The buyer must also occupy the home within nine months. Our [first-time buyers guide](/first-time-home-buyers-toronto/) sets out the other conditions.

**Non-resident taxes.** A buyer who is not a citizen or permanent resident pays Ontario's 25 percent non-resident speculation tax and, in Toronto, the City's 10 percent municipal version on certain residential properties. A lawyer should review your situation before any offer.

**Property tax and the Vacant Home Tax.** The 2026 residential rate in Toronto totals 0.767311 percent of assessed value. That is $767.31 per $100,000 of assessment. Every owner must also declare the home's occupancy each year. A home left empty for six months or more of a year, for example while a family is still abroad, can be charged the Vacant Home Tax at 3 percent of assessed value. Our post on [Toronto property tax](/blog/toronto-property-tax/) explains the bill.

## Schools

In Ontario the home address determines which school a child is assigned to. Confirm the boundary with the board, in writing if you can, for the exact address before you buy. Boundaries change and a listing is not a reliable source for them.

## A first year, in order

1. Apply for OHIP in person as soon as you arrive.
2. Exchange or replace your driving licence within 60 days, with your driving history documents ready.
3. Rent somewhere with good transit so you can explore easily.
4. Register children with the board that serves your rental address.
5. Try your likely commutes from two or three candidate areas at rush hour.
6. Line up mortgage preapproval once your income can be documented here, using our [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator-toronto/) as a first estimate.
7. Buy once you know which area fits, not because a calendar says so.
